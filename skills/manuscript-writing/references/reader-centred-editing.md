# Reader-centred scientific editing

A selective diagnostic for manuscript-writing. Adapted from reusable
scientific-writing principles in Research Manuscript Verifier. This is a
reference, **not** a competing manuscript-prose skill.

## Diagnose before rewriting

1. **Audience and job:** Who needs to understand this passage? What should
   the reader know, infer or decide afterward? For a genuinely unfamiliar
   community before major reframing, use discourse-community-mapping.
2. **Value and contribution:** Does the paragraph establish a meaningful
   uncertainty, comparison or useful result? A gap alone is not significance.
   Do not invent scholarly conflict or inflate practical consequences.
3. **Reader order:** Is the logic organised for the reader or by the researcher's
   discovery chronology? Preserve reproducible procedure in Methods.
4. **Paragraph focus:** Read the main subjects of consecutive sentences.
   Do they track the scientific entity, relationship, evidence or question?
   Changes to 'we', 'studies', or ambiguous 'this' may break the focus chain,
   but author subjects and passive voice can also be correct.
5. **Common ground:** Explain only what these readers do not already know.
   Retain efficient specialist terminology. Repair missing antecedents,
   assumptions and unexplained inference jumps.
6. **Sentence stress:** Make the important new information easy to locate,
   often near the sentence end. Keep the proper scientific subject. Pair
   abstract claims with specific, true examples only if they improve clarity.
7. **Argument integrity:** Verify apparent consensus, 'first', 'however',
   causal verbs, novelty, uncertainty and implications when editing changes
   their scientific meaning. Represent prior work fairly, including dissent.
8. **Voice and scope:** Compare adjacent approved prose. Keep taxon, region,
   comparison, estimate, uncertainty, terminology and citation placement.

## Use proportionately

- Use the relevant tests only for an actual clarity, flow or framing problem.
- For major Intro/Discussion work, diagnose contribution and paragraph order
  before polishing sentences.
- For literature reviews, synthesise relationships and counterevidence
  instead of listing papers chronologically.
- For copy-edits that preserve meaning, no exhaustive new literature audit
  is necessary; verify only materially altered scientific assertions.
- Keep an effective passage unchanged. No mandatory checklist theatre.

## Output

Return concise revised prose in the manuscript's own voice and only material
issues or unresolved evidence limits. Do not invent problems to justify edits.
