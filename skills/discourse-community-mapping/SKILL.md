# Discourse-community mapping

Produce a source-backed reader codebook to guide manuscript positioning. A
**code of value** links a question the audience recognises, why its answer
matters, the evidence it expects, and the language used to express it.
Repeated words, citation counts, and journal prestige do not prove value or
consensus.

## Use when

- Framing a new manuscript for a particular field or journal.
- Substantially revising Introduction, significance, or Discussion for readers
  whose priorities or evidence standards are uncertain.
- Positioning interdisciplinary work for more than one reader community.
- Explicitly asked to map disciplinary terminology, concepts, or readership.

Do not run for routine copy-edits, isolated reviewer comments, or small
Methods/Results wording changes. Reuse an existing source-backed codebook
when the audience and question have not changed.

## Workflow

1. **Define readers and decision.** Record the actual research question,
   section, intended audience(s), and named journal if provided. Without
   a journal, label the target audience provisional.
2. **Sample deliberately.** Inspect a justified small set of target-journal
   papers, key studies cited by the manuscript, recent empirical and
   synthesis papers, and relevant contradictory or boundary cases. Expand
   only where disagreement affects the decision; record coverage limits.
3. **Read the arguments.** Examine actual Introductions, Discussions, and
   relevant Methods of accessible original full texts. Abstracts and
   snippets are only preliminary orientation. For data claims, inspect
   relevant Results, tables, figures and supplements.
4. **Extract meaning, not keywords.** For each pattern record the reader's
   question, scientific distinction or comparison, expected evidence,
   authentic terminology in context, consequence, paper and locator.
   Distinguish what authors explicitly say from editorial synthesis.
5. **Test boundaries.** Record material taxon, setting, methodological,
   time-period and disciplinary differences. Include contrary examples;
   do not call a small convenience sample a consensus.
6. **Map to manuscript.** Which codes does the manuscript already serve?
   Which reader questions go unanswered? Recommend the fewest useful
   changes with exact manuscript locations and required evidence checks.
7. **Hand off.** Send the codebook to manuscript-writing for actual prose
   changes. Send any new scientific, causal, novelty or consensus claim to
   fact-check before it is asserted. Do not silently edit manuscript files.

## Evidence and output

Use [the codebook template](references/codebook.md) proportionately. For
each source distinguish access (full text inspected, partial original text,
abstract only, metadata only, or unavailable) from status (observed framing,
defensible synthesis, provisional interpretation).

Return target readers, sampling rationale, compact source/locator codebook,
material dissent, and ranked framing opportunities. Never invent page numbers,
quotes, reviewer preferences, or supporting studies.

## Ownership

- This skill owns **audience research and codebook**, not prose editing.
- manuscript-writing owns manuscript structure, paragraphs and voice.
- fact-check owns claim-to-evidence verification.
- literature-review owns review corpus and evidence synthesis.
- manuscript-submission owns official journal instructions.

A source-backed audience preference cannot make a scientific claim true.
