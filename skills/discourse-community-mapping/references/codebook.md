# Reader codebook template

Use only as much detail as the framing decision warrants.

- Manuscript question and target section:
- Journal and primary / adjacent readers:

## Audience codes

| Reader question | Scientific distinction | Expected evidence | Authentic terminology | Paper and exact section/figure locator | Access and status | Limits or dissent | Manuscript location and decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What must the reader assess? | Comparison, mechanism, uncertainty | Study design, denominators, uncertainty, validation | Meaningful domain term, not buzzword | DOI/link and locator | Full / partial / abstract; observed / inferred | Domain boundary and counterexample | Keep / clarify / verify / move / omit |

## Synthesis

1. Mark directly observed framing separately from interpretive synthesis.
2. Show audience differences rather than forcing cross-field consensus.
3. Prioritise only useful, evidence-consistent positioning opportunities.
4. For any newly proposed factual, consensus, novelty or causal claim,
   mark it for independent fact-checking before manuscript insertion.

Do not turn a convenience sample into a claim about every journal reader.
A vocabulary list alone is not a codebook.
